Stop — please read this

Buying fake reviews is illegal, and the penalties fall on you

Nothing is for sale on this website. No reviews are sold here and no payments are accepted. This page exists to reach people at the moment they consider buying fake reviews — because most never see what actually happens next.

Purchasing fake reviews is unlawful across the United States, the United Kingdom and the European Union. It has already produced a criminal conviction and a prison sentence.

  • United States. The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule carries civil penalties of up to $53,088 per violation — and a package of reviews can constitute many violations, not one.
  • United Kingdom. Under the DMCC Act 2024 the CMA can fine a business up to 10% of annual global turnover, imposed directly without going to court.
  • European Union. Commissioning fake reviews is a blacklisted practice, prohibited in all circumstances, with no assessment of harm or intent.
  • Italy, 2018. The operator of a review-selling business was sentenced to nine months' imprisonment plus €8,000 in costs.

Platform penalties arrive separately, and usually months after the seller has been paid and become uncontactable: reviews removed, ranking penalties lasting a year, disqualification from awards, a public warning badge on your own listing where your customers can see it, and in some cases suspension of your profile — which can take your genuine reviews with it.

Every guarantee on the page behind this notice is one a seller cannot honour. No seller controls whether a platform removes a review, detection is applied retrospectively to content already published, and a contract to commit an unlawful act is unenforceable anywhere. The seller keeps your money, your listing URL, and documentary proof of what you commissioned.

The Coalition for Trusted Reviews

A cross-industry group founded in 2023 to safeguard trustworthy consumer reviews worldwide. Members include Amazon, Booking.com, Expedia Group, G2, HolidayCheck, Tripadvisor and Trustpilot, who share intelligence on fraudulent operators with one another.

  • Ask every customer for a review, not only the ones you expect to be positive.
  • Use your platform's own review-request tools — those reviews can't be taken away.
  • Never offer discounts, prizes or refunds in exchange for a review.
  • If a competitor's rating looks implausible, report it. Platforms act on reports.
