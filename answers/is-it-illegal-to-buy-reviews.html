Is it illegal to buy online reviews?

Last reviewed 30 August 2026

Yes. Buying fake reviews is unlawful in the United States, the United Kingdom, the European Union and Australia, and has already produced a criminal conviction and a prison sentence.

Liability falls on the business that commissions the reviews, not only on whoever writes them.

The rules, in short

WhereWhat appliesMaximum exposure
United States FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, in force since October 2024 Civil penalties of up to $53,088 per violation
United Kingdom Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, in force since April 2025 10% of annual global turnover, or £300,000 — whichever is higher, imposed by the CMA directly without going to court
European Union Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, Annex I — the blacklist Prohibited in all circumstances, with no assessment of harm or intent. Penalties set per member state
Italy Criminal law — the first conviction anywhere in the world for review fraud, 2018 Nine months’ imprisonment plus €8,000 in costs and damages

“Per violation” is the phrase that matters

The FTC ceiling is not per business or per order. A course of conduct involving many reviews can be treated as many violations, so exposure scales with the volume bought. This is why the arithmetic on a cheap review package is so poor: the downside is nothing like proportionate to the spend.

The rules are broader than most buyers expect

Alongside buying and selling fake reviews, the same regimes also prohibit:

  • Review gating — asking for reviews only from customers you expect to be positive. No review here is fake, and it is still a violation.
  • Insider reviews without clear disclosure — including from owners, staff, family or agencies.
  • Suppressing negative reviews on your own site or channels.
  • Buying followers or engagement to misrepresent influence.

“I only bought them, I didn’t write them”

This is the most common misconception, and it is wrong everywhere above. The rules target commissioning fake reviews; the EU blacklist names it explicitly, and the FTC rule covers buying as well as creating.

In practice the buyer is often the easier enforcement target. A business is identifiable, locally regulated, holds assets and has a reputation to lose. Sellers are frequently offshore operations effectively beyond the reach of a regulator. When a review-selling network is dismantled, its customer list is a list of identifiable, reachable, liable businesses.

The seller carries none of this. The regulatory exposure, the platform penalties and the reputational damage all attach to your business. The seller has already been paid, has no listing to penalise, and faces no consequence when your reviews are removed.
