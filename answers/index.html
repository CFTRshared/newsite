Buying Online Reviews: The Facts

An awareness resource from the Coalition for Trusted Reviews

What actually happens when a business buys online reviews

Buying reviews is unlawful in the United States, the United Kingdom and the European Union, and has produced a criminal conviction and a prison sentence in Italy. Detection is retroactive, so "guaranteed" and "non-drop" reviews are routinely removed months after payment. The penalties land on the business that bought them, not on the seller.

If you have already paid someone for reviews, start here.

Most people who search for review sellers are not fraudsters. They are business owners with too few reviews, a competitor whose rating looks suspiciously good, and no obvious way to catch up. The sales pitch is designed for exactly that situation: fast, cheap, low-risk, everyone does it.

The pitch is wrong on the risk. This section sets out what the record actually shows — the specific laws, the detection mechanics, the penalty schedules, and the pattern of what happens to buyers after they pay. All of it is sourced.

What to do instead

The underlying problem — not enough reviews — has legitimate solutions, and they are more effective than purchased reviews because they don't get deleted.

  • Ask every customer, every time. Most satisfied customers will leave a review if asked directly and made it easy. Very few will do it unprompted. This single change closes most review-count gaps.
  • Use the platform's own tools. Most major review platforms provide free review-request links, QR codes, email templates or widgets. These are sanctioned, and reviews collected this way are never at risk of removal.
  • Ask everyone, not just the happy ones. Filtering requests to customers you expect to be positive — "review gating" — is itself a violation under the FTC rule and the DMCC Act, even though no review is fake. Ask your whole customer base.
  • Never offer anything in exchange — no discounts, entries into prize draws, free products or refunds — unless the platform explicitly permits it and the incentive is disclosed in the review.
  • Report the competitor you're trying to catch up with. If a rival's rating looks implausible, report it. Platforms act on these reports, and it addresses the actual problem rather than adding to it.

Reporting review fraud

If you have been approached by a review seller, have been threatened by one, or believe a competitor is buying reviews, report it to the platform concerned. Every Coalition member operates a content-integrity team that acts on these reports, and members share intelligence on fraudulent operators with one another.
