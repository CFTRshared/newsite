Can you get caught buying reviews?

Last reviewed 30 August 2026

Yes, and routinely. By way of example, Tripadvisor (a co-founding member of the Coalition) alone identified more than 1.3 million fake review submissions in a single year, and applied ranking penalties to more than 33,000 businesses.

The risk is not yours to manage

Buying reviews means putting your listing in the hands of an operation you cannot verify — one with every commercial incentive to tell you it is safe, and none to tell you when it stops being safe. Whatever assurances you were given, you have no way to check them and no recourse when they fail.

Detection is also retroactive. Reviews that appear this week can be removed long after you have paid and the seller has stopped replying, and the penalty attaches to your business, not to theirs. There is no point at which purchased reviews become safe.

Getting caught once can mean getting caught everywhere

Since 2023, the major review platforms have shared intelligence on review fraud with one another through the Coalition for Trusted Reviews. Members include Amazon, Booking.com, Expedia Group, G2, HolidayCheck, Tripadvisor and Trustpilot. One of the Coalition’s four stated areas of focus is bad actor intelligence — sharing information on how fraudulent operators work, including companies that sell fake reviews to businesses.

The practical consequence for a buyer: a business operating across several platforms is not taking one isolated risk per platform. It is taking one risk that can surface on all of them.
