What happens if you buy fake reviews?

Last reviewed 30 August 2026

The consequences arrive in sequence, not all at once, and typically long after the seller has been paid and stopped replying.

Every one of them attaches to your business. None of them attach to the seller.

What the platforms do

ConsequenceWhat it means in practice
Reviews removed or blocked The mildest outcome, and close to inevitable. You have paid for something with no residual value while keeping all the liability that came with it.
Ranking penalty Applied to the listing and able to last for months. A business that buys reviews and is caught does not return to where it started — it ends up below it, having paid for the privilege.
Award disqualification Loss of eligibility for platform awards and recognition. For hospitality and travel businesses these badges drive real booking volume.
Public warning badge In severe cases, a notice on your own listing telling customers that suspicious review activity has been identified. The purchase was meant to make you look trustworthy; it ends with a warning label your customers can read.
Profile suspension On some platforms the listing itself goes. For a local business that means losing its primary discovery channel, and reinstatement can cost you your genuine reviews and ranking history too.

The scale is not marginal. In a single year Tripadvisor applied ranking penalties to more than 33,000 businesses for fraud, and issued hundreds of public warning badges.

What regulators do, separately

Platform enforcement and regulatory enforcement are different tracks. One does not substitute for the other, and a single course of conduct can produce both.

WhereMaximum exposure
United States — FTCUp to $53,088 per violation, and many reviews can mean many violations
United Kingdom — CMA10% of annual global turnover or £300,000, imposed directly without going to court
European UnionA blacklisted practice, prohibited in all circumstances
ItalyPrecedent exists: nine months’ imprisonment plus €8,000 in costs

The arithmetic

A review package costs a few tens or hundreds of dollars. Against that: the money gone, everything you bought removed, a ranking penalty lasting months, awards eligibility lost, a public warning badge on your listing, possible suspension of a profile that may take your genuine reviews with it, and regulatory exposure that scales with the number of reviews you bought.

And the seller keeps your money, your listing URL, your email address and proof of what you commissioned. What tends to happen next is on the next page.
